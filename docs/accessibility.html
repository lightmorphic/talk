Accessibility

Last updated 6 September 2026.

Our aim

This website is intended to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at level AA.

What we have done

  • Every page works from the keyboard alone, and the focus outline is visible against the background at every step.
  • Text and background colours were measured for contrast rather than eyeballed, and meet the level AA thresholds.
  • Headings are in order, landmarks are marked up, and each page starts with a skip link.
  • Links and buttons have touch targets of at least 44 by 44 pixels.
  • Illustrations are drawn as inline graphics with text alternatives; those that are decorative are hidden from screen readers.
  • The layout reflows to a narrow screen without horizontal scrolling, down to 320 pixels wide.
  • Nothing moves, flashes or auto-plays.

Known limitations

The grid-of-dots launcher in the top bar, which lists our other applications, is supplied by a shared script rather than written for this page. We believe it to be keyboard operable, but it has not been through the same testing as the rest of the site.

The application

Lightmorphic Talk itself exists to help people who find typing difficult or slow. It is operated by clicking one button, has no keyboard shortcuts to memorise, and offers three sizes of that button. Its interface has not been formally audited against a desktop accessibility standard, and it does not yet have a full screen-reader-only workflow.

Telling us about a problem

If something on this site or in the application shuts you out, we want to know, and we will fix it. Write to complaints@lightmorphic.com and say what you were trying to do and what got in the way.
